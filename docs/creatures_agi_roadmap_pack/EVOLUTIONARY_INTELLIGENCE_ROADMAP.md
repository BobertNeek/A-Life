# Evolutionary Intelligence Roadmap

**Status:** Long-range approved direction; implementation remains promotion-gated

**Date:** 2026-07-28

**Design authority:**
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-28-evolutionary-intelligence-selection-design.md`

## 1. Goal

Build a fun artificial-life game in which embodied creatures learn, reproduce,
form societies, and become more intelligent through natural and artificial
selection. Begin with Norn-plus early mammals. Progress through pack and
hunter-gatherer cognition. Finish the consumer-hardware path by educating one
rare ascended creature at the largest comfortable local brain scale.

AGI remains speculative. The roadmap measures concrete behavior and never uses
future ambition as evidence of present capability.

## 2. Starting point

The completed backend provides:

- GPU-authoritative closed-loop neural behavior;
- N512, N1024, and N2048 production capacity classes;
- the first trained N2048 foundation;
- grounded perception and limited neural speech;
- Hebbian/Oja waking plasticity and sleep consolidation;
- bounded memory and topology sidecars;
- persistent neural addresses and research N4096 growth;
- lineage archives and founder modes.

Before the next major brain program, complete the planned visual and player-
experience overhaul. This does not change neural authority.

## 3. Rules that survive every era

1. World systems provide legal unscored candidates and measured outcomes.
2. The GPU brain selects neural actions. No CPU neural shadow or silent fallback
   may return.
3. Ordinary offspring inherit DNA and foundation identity, not personal memory.
4. Teachers and players communicate through ordinary perception.
5. Tests record assistance, exposure, provenance, adapter, foundation, and
   lineage.
6. Fixed benchmark answers never dominate breeding.
7. Brain size has energy, sleep, reproduction, and population costs.
8. Larger classes require causal, behavioral, save, soak, and hardware gates.
9. Wild and managed creatures use the same brain and body simulation.
10. Each era uses the smallest brain class that meets its target.

## 4. Cross-era systems

### 4.1 Diploid DNA and phenotype expression

Create a composite `CreatureGenome` for body, brain, chemistry, development,
reproduction, and inherited predispositions. Support two alleles, crossover,
bounded mutation, continuous blending, and discrete dominance. Activate each
currently declared genome family only after it has a causal phenotype mapping.

### 4.2 Biochemistry and development

Connect body state, drives, hormones, neural modulation, critical periods,
sleep, reproduction, and developmental migration. Keep chemistry bounded and
multi-rate. Chemistry influences cognition but never emits actions.

### 4.3 Multi-level selection

Maintain ecological, cognitive, social, group, stability, efficiency, and
diversity objectives. Use natural breeding, managed pairing, cognitive
introgression, and probation cohorts. Preserve wild reservoirs and archived
lineages.

### 4.4 Intelligence batteries

Maintain permanent anchor tests, procedural breeding tests, and hidden
promotion tests. Rotate domains and layouts. Measure learning, transfer,
reversal, delayed memory, abstraction, social contribution, and compute cost.

### 4.5 Habitat management

Allow wild, reserve, managed, and school habitats in one world. Track transfers,
quarantine, breeding authority, teaching, foundation use, and later possession.

### 4.6 Foundation distillation

At approved era boundaries, distill robust population-wide improvements into a
new audited foundation. Keep ordinary births Baldwinian. Preserve older
foundations for experiments and rollback.

### 4.7 Product and research workstreams retained from the legacy roadmap

The legacy CA roadmap contained useful work that remains necessary, without its
obsolete baseline or milestone machinery:

- player controls, camera, selection, visible intent, and recovery;
- player-facing save/load, environment setup, and sandbox editing;
- resource ecology, terrain zones, lifecycle, reproduction, death, and lineage;
- school presentation, embodied teachers, curriculum authoring, and exact
  verifiers;
- memory, topology, chemistry, neural activity, and behavior comparison tools;
- GPU telemetry, population batching, bounded performance, and long soaks;
- coherent art, animation, drive-coupled audio and effects, and onboarding;
- Windows packaging, prerequisite diagnostics, crash capture, external testing,
  and release review;
- culture, observational learning, curiosity, world-model research, continual
  learning, self-modeling, planning, and causal ablations.

These are workstreams inside the eras below. They are not a second numbered
roadmap and do not override promotion gates.

## 5. Era 0: Playable ecosystem and selection laboratory

**Purpose:** Make the existing creature world enjoyable and create the tools
needed to observe evolution.

### Deliverables

- Complete the approved visual overhaul.
- Finish camera, selection, visible-intent, reset, and recovery interactions.
- Make creature actions, speech, needs, relationships, and learning readable.
- Provide player-facing save/load, environment setup, and sandbox editing.
- Provide wild, reserve, managed, and school habitat controls.
- Complete resource ecology, terrain zones, lifecycle, reproduction, and death.
- Add pedigree, chemistry, cognition, and provenance inspection.
- Add memory, topology, neural activity, and behavior-comparison views.
- Add test arenas and replayable intelligence trials.
- Provide stable packaging, diagnostics, crash capture, and tester feedback.
- Establish hardware-based population budgets.

### Exit gate

The player can run, observe, save, breed, test, archive, and compare a stable
multi-generation population without hidden policy control.

## 6. Era 1: Norn-plus early mammal

**Target:** Approach lab-rat-level adaptive behavior in simplified worlds while
exceeding Norn-style basic speech.

### Cognitive targets

- self-maintenance and flexible foraging;
- predator and hazard avoidance;
- spatial navigation and remembered locations;
- delayed choice and post-sleep retention;
- reward reversal;
- object and layout generalization;
- simple multi-step problem solving;
- individual recognition;
- imitation and basic social learning;
- inherited starter vocabulary and learned grounded words.

### Evolution targets

- activate evolvable chemistry, drive, development, and neuron-dynamics genes;
- implement diploid crossover and bounded mutation;
- establish wild and managed comparison populations;
- run periodic individual and group intelligence tests;
- exercise cognitive introgression for high-IQ, low-survival individuals;
- prove gains across generations and unseen environments.

### Game features

- player-built habitats and test chambers;
- selective breeding and natural mating;
- visible family trees and trait comparisons;
- creature speech, names, bonds, fears, preferences, and habits;
- reintroduction of managed lineages into wild reserves.

### Promotion gate

The population must outperform plasticity-disabled, memory-disabled, sleep-
disabled, and non-social controls. Gains must persist across seeds, lineages,
held-out worlds, and reproduction. Brain scaling begins only after a measured
plateau.

## 7. Era 2: Social mammal and proto-pack

**Target:** Stable social groups with transferable social intelligence.

### Cognitive targets

- kin and individual recognition;
- attachment, trust, fear, rivalry, and reconciliation;
- observational learning and intentional teaching;
- shared alarm and resource signals;
- dominance, leadership, following, and role specialization;
- cooperative defense and group movement;
- detection of free riders and unreliable signals;
- group memory that survives member turnover.

### Selection design

- evaluate persistent family groups and randomized teams;
- run removal and replacement trials;
- select teachers, coordinators, specialists, and group multipliers;
- preserve individual competence while rewarding group success;
- maintain genetic and cultural diversity between packs.

### Promotion gate

Group performance must exceed the sum of isolated members. Social skills must
transfer to unfamiliar partners. Pack culture must survive without hidden
teacher state or scripted coordination.

## 8. Era 3: Pack intelligence and coordinated hunting

**Target:** Flexible group plans in changing ecological conditions.

### Cognitive targets

- coordinated pursuit, ambush, defense, and retreat;
- role assignment based on ability and condition;
- route selection and shared spatial objectives;
- delayed cooperation and reciprocal aid;
- food sharing, caching, and conflict resolution;
- communication about absent targets and future actions;
- adaptation when prey, terrain, or competitors change.

### Artificial selection

- select both successful packs and high-contribution individuals;
- use procedurally changing prey and terrain to defeat fixed hunting scripts;
- reward transfer, recovery from failed plans, and efficient coordination;
- penalize neural and metabolic waste.

### Promotion gate

Packs must solve unfamiliar coordination problems without fixed role scripts.
Lesions to communication, memory, or social learning must reduce performance in
predicted ways.

## 9. Era 4: Hunter-gatherer proto-culture

**Target:** Persistent culture, simple tools, division of labor, and shared
plans.

### Cognitive targets

- tool discovery, manufacture, reuse, and teaching;
- gathering, hunting, storage, shelter, and seasonal planning;
- family and group roles that can change with circumstance;
- norms, reputation, exchange, punishment, and reconciliation;
- compositional speech about objects, events, agents, places, and intentions;
- cultural traditions that outlive their inventors;
- transfer of skills between groups and generations.

### Game features

- settlements, resource territories, schools, rituals, and material culture;
- interpretable dialects and cultural histories;
- player diplomacy through ordinary speech and demonstrations;
- managed cultural exchange between wild and selected populations.

### Promotion gate

Groups must preserve and improve useful behavior through social transmission.
Removing direct artificial teaching must not erase established culture within
one generation.

## 10. Era 5: Abstract developmental generalist

**Target:** Move from practical culture toward explicit abstraction and formal
education.

### School progression

1. object labels, actions, safety, and self-report;
2. quantities, comparison, sequence, and spatial relations;
3. visible symbols, reading, and writing;
4. arithmetic and exact logic with deterministic verifiers;
5. simulated history, ecology, causal science, and tool design;
6. explanation, teaching, planning, and counterfactual tasks.

### Cognitive additions

- expanded working and episodic memory;
- learned world-model prediction;
- multi-step planning and dream rehearsal;
- symbolic scratch space;
- self-monitoring and uncertainty reporting;
- continual learning without routine catastrophic forgetting.

### Selection pressure

Select learning efficiency, transfer, explanation fidelity, teaching ability,
and novel problem solving. Do not select polished language alone. Every abstract
claim must connect to exact verification or grounded action.

### Promotion gate

Creatures must transfer concepts across domains and solve held-out problems that
cannot be passed through memorized language patterns.

## 11. Era 6: Late-game individual ascension

**Target:** Grow one exceptional evolved creature to the largest brain that runs
comfortably on the user's machine.

### Candidate selection

- require strong individual cognition, stable personality, social competence,
  learning efficiency, and health;
- allow an exceptional high-IQ candidate with survival weaknesses through a
  documented support plan;
- preserve the source creature and rollback checkpoint.

### Migration ladder

Use only promoted classes. The current near-term step is research N4096.
Possible later research classes include 32K, 131K, 1M, and 5M. These names are
compatibility targets, not promises. Each step needs measured memory, latency,
save, learning, and behavioral evidence.

### Identity preservation

- map the evolved core by persistent address;
- preserve consolidated memory, chemistry, habits, language, and provenance;
- add expansion capacity dormant;
- train new regions through critical periods and staged education;
- let new cognition influence action only through normal arbitration;
- retain rollback until the migrated mind passes identity and behavior checks.

### Player possession

Optional possession may appear here or late in Era 5. It provides assisted
embodied demonstrations and game variety. It never becomes unmarked training
data or permanent direct action authority.

## 12. Era 7: Advanced education and model comparison

**Target:** Determine what the ascended creature can learn and how its approach
differs from conventional models.

### Evaluation domains

- continual grounded learning;
- planning and tool use;
- language comprehension and generation;
- memory across long histories;
- mathematics and formal logic;
- causal and scientific reasoning;
- social understanding and teaching;
- novel-task transfer;
- robustness after changing rules or goals.

Compare against suitable conventional models under matched tools, context, and
compute where possible. Record different strengths instead of forcing one
misleading aggregate score. Similarity to a large language model is an empirical
question, not a roadmap promise.

## 13. Era 8: Commercial game and compute flywheel

If the game is fun and the research results are compelling:

1. Ship the creature game with wild, reserve, managed, school, and lineage play.
2. Make research claims narrow, reproducible, and evidence-backed.
3. Use revenue to fund better consumer hardware, servers, or research compute.
4. Promote larger classes only after hardware and behavioral evidence.
5. Keep local single-player play functional without mandatory cloud cognition.
6. Preserve the same sensory, action, genome, chemistry, and provenance rules on
   future backends.

Commercial success may fund scaling. It does not relax scientific honesty or
neural authority.

## 14. Population and compute policy

Population caps are empirical:

```text
population cap = min(GPU neural budget, world simulation budget, memory budget)
```

Each brain-class promotion runs populated benchmarks on the target hardware.
The game then reduces active population while preserving a viable breeding
cohort and several lineages. Cold or distant creatures may use lower simulation
cadence, but they never switch to a different brain policy.

Late-game saves may contain many archived creatures, a small live support
population, and one expensive ascended individual.

## 15. Preventing evolutionary shortcuts

- Procedurally vary worlds, objects, rewards, layouts, and social partners.
- Keep promotion tests hidden from ordinary training.
- Track test exposure and teacher assistance.
- Require transfer and reversal, not final score alone.
- Charge energy for brain size and activity.
- Retain wild comparison populations.
- Protect minority lineages and useful specialists.
- Reject hidden semantic labels in grounded evaluation.
- Use causal lesions and ablations.
- Treat suspicious sudden gains as hypotheses to investigate.

Evolution will cheat. The player is allowed to cheat too. The receipts must say
which clever bastard did it.

## 16. Decision gates

Stop and review before:

- enabling a new evolvable gene family;
- publishing a new foundation;
- promoting a larger brain class;
- allowing inherited epigenetics or Lamarckian carryover;
- introducing formal pack selection;
- adding tools or culture scoring;
- beginning abstract schooling;
- ascending a living creature;
- claiming comparison with a conventional model;
- changing hardware backend or using remote compute;
- making commercial cognitive claims.

Each gate needs a concrete artifact, held-out evidence, ablations, provenance,
hardware cost, and rollback.

## 17. Near-term order

2. Write the Era 1 implementation plan from the approved design.
3. Activate DNA, chemistry, development, and selection foundations.
4. Add habitat-level wild, reserve, managed, and school authority.

Later eras stay documented but dormant until their preceding gate passes.
