# Next hypothesis: learn an operation's physical efficacy, not a food's general badness

No new run or gain change is proposed automatically. Preserve the completed comparison.

## Grounding in the receipts

Across all four lives, the60 unsuccessful operation receipts consist entirely of25 H/Play failures and35 Vocalize failures. Close/Release and ingestion did not make up this failed-operation set. Both arms ate each H only once, handled H repeatedly, and continued obtaining13 safe meals per life. Thus these observations support investigating action-specific motor efficacy more directly than increasing aversion to handling H.

The clearest measured example is2602/157: Close succeeded in acquiring H, while substituted Play failed and left H unheld; S was unavailable. ON later acquired H anyway. This is a difference between operations on the same object, not evidence that the object should be avoided. A poisonous-to-eat object can be safe and useful to carry, move or place.

## Hypothesis

A context-sensitive prediction of an operation's physical success, updated from its own efference/outcome receipts, could reduce repeated ineffective attempts while retaining curiosity and useful handling. Keep predictions separate for exact operation and relevant body state, including heldness, reach and available effector. A failed Play attempt should not automatically penalize successful Close on that same target. Consumed-event pain/nutrient credit belongs to the consumption prediction; noninjurious grasp/transport should remain distinguishable.

This is a hypothesis, not an established missing circuit. Existing exact-Play/boredom work may already supply part of this signal; audit that work rather than duplicating it.

## Source-only audit first

Trace why the35 Vocalize and25 H/Play attempts failed and whether the current candidate/predictor already represents that reason. A Vocalize candidate with no usable utterance plan, or an operation known infeasible from the creature's actual available motor plan, may be a candidate-construction defect rather than something to teach through punishment. Do not assume failure means distress, inject an arbitrary penalty, read hidden Food/H labels as a policy oracle, or penalize a temporarily unreachable target as permanently useless.

If an existing physical-success predictor is correctly wired but the failure is upstream feasibility, fix the concrete feasibility mismatch. If valid repeatable attempts fail yet the policy lacks operation-specific outcome information, test the reader/predictor hypothesis. Distinguish uncertainty from learned failure so exploratory rechecking remains possible.

## Falsifiable controls for a later authorized test

Start with fixed ordinary physical fixtures: successful Play on a playable object; failed Play in the same observed/body context; successful Close and task-conditioned Release of the same harmless-to-touch object; ingested harm versus benign handling; transient unreachable versus newly reachable state; unrelated-target and empty-memory controls. Test actual operating-point components and causal identity before any fresh behavior allocation. Do not invent unit utility margins or choose a gain just to fit them.

The desired specificity is fewer repeated ineffective operation attempts without reducing successful play, useful transport, necessary release or safe-food intake. Mere reductions in H handling or increases in score movement do not satisfy this criterion. A separate goal-conditioned transport task is needed to evaluate useful handling; the completed feeding assay does not supply that evidence.

This route is more biologically grounded than unconditional Ingest-valence transfer because it associates motor plans with experienced physical consequences and interoceptive outcomes at the operation that produced them. It still requires source verification and prospective evidence, and no additional study has been allocated or launched.
