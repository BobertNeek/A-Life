# Four-cell visual trace, research only

This records a disposable CPU experiment on fresh experimental brains. It is not a production sensory ABI, GPU implementation, trained-checkpoint migration, or demonstrated map/maze competence. No accepted brain or actor/value optimizer state was modified.

## Circuit

Two cue cells and their two reciprocal partners were isolated from other inherited recurrent inputs, retaining reciprocal self-excitation and cross-inhibition. Two authored readout paths connect them to left/right turn scoring. An explicitly temporary CPU input supplies the direction of a currently visible blue-biased object; color is a fixture for the experiment, not a general food detector or a hidden-target oracle. The candidate directly edits 660 genetic weights relative to its reference. The adjacent patch captures experimental input, host intervention, probe and recipe; it is not eligible for production use as-is. A real implementation needs a versioned grounded input contract, saved-state identity, CPU/GPU parity and live validation.

In a detached frozen-frame probe, either synthetic directional impulse persisted through 64 replay steps, and eight frames of the opposite input switched the turn-logit sign. This does not establish live memory or performance.

## Live causal test

A wide physical occluder is transparent initially and becomes opaque after decision 4. In the cue condition the target was visible exactly on decisions 1–4 and never thereafter; in the blind condition it was never visible. Paired sham and intervention runs share their exact first four decision rows. At that completed boundary the intervention zeros the active and inactive activations of only four specified cells, uses normal canonical resealing, and cold-restores; no physics, reward or food is fabricated. The fixed input after occlusion is not guaranteed identical across the two separate lives: initial physical trajectories and other neural state can differ.

For isolated-cell seed 1862, resetting those cells changed 38 later forced-slot vectors in the cue condition and none in the blind condition. On fresh seeds 1863 and 1864 the corresponding counts were 27 and 21 in cue, and zero in blind. All 12 original branch checks and all 8 replication cases had exact cold-next-step checks; required target visibility was verified. The later cue-versus-blind differences were not eliminated in the two replications (36 and 47 later vectors still differed after reset), so other state or early physical differences also contribute. This supports a causal cue-dependent behavioral influence through these cells in this fixture, not a learned world map, proof of food concept, or useful maze solution.

A separate seed 1860 live check with the temporary cue: all three variants ate clear and neutral food and did not consume the bitter object (configured pain 0.3) in 120 decisions, but all missed both initially hidden targets in 240-decision mirrored mazes. One seed is not a general safety or performance benchmark.
