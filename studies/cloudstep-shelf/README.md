# Cloudstep Shelf: a forest window onto the real high island

The original island has a surprisingly quiet 57–73 m forest shelf between its seaward scarp and 240 m summit. The 60 m square around the outer face spans **91.756 m of relief**. Within the larger survey 2,186 connected one-metre sample centres have a grade below 0.44 and lie at shelf height. Fourteen originally planted conifers lie on that patch. This is a terrain and composition survey, **not a tested walkable area**. No map, save, root, collider or native render setting changes.

The proposed creature-height summit frame is at x82, z−408, **1.04 m** above a true 0.049-grade V1 triangle. An actual opaque-geometry/alpha-cutout ray from there to the original 240 m summit at (28.125, 240, −275) misses all sampled original props; the land also stays below it. The line left toward the foothill instead meets the original #533 spruce about 4.94 m from the eye. Moving the camera just over five metres to (80, −413) places that *same real spruce* across the summit. Leave it there. The steep uphill look and the close forest floor will be separate camera attitudes, **not a fabricated view containing both**. The opposite sea-level witness is already blocked by over ten metres of native ground.

**Art direction for the next full pass.** Frame the real, root-dense foreground with the current original canopy library. Give the 90 m wall and the ascent behind it a grounded rhythm of dark rain-cut stone, broken pale inclusions and broad surviving mantle; allow the real shape to set their direction rather than painting horizontal belts. Keep the calm, lighter opening between the sheltered trunks and the angular rock above. Use one native-contour guardian view to retain the shelf’s full structure; from the 1.04 m eye the existing left spruce and true summit should stay legible. The mapped original BoulderC #1330, near #991, is one genuine foreground anchor, not a request to scatter more rocks.

`profile.py NATIVE OUTPUT` writes the original-root survey, 1,330 directly sampled triangle stations and the height/grade register. `sightlines.py NATIVE OUTPUT` writes the exact five eyes, original crest/shoulder witnesses and triangle/alpha hits. The current files are source scouting, **not yet a rendered or native-integrated visual change**.

**First optical source.** The camera register changes the art priority: a full 77.1% of the summit-eye's 1,026 terrain contacts are on grade above 0.75, but the underfoot view looks down at 2–6 m of gentle pine floor. Three differently dipping 3D planes are attached to actual rock around (71.74, −391.07), (65.12, −385.10), and (86.70, −357.20). Their broken pale margins cover 13.94% of the uphill view's ground hits, but none of the gentle floor. The guardian sees a quieter 1.52% exposure. The candidate intentionally leaves that close floor for a distinct, authentically rooted treatment. These are ground-only source coverage figures, not rendered pictures or visibility past all foliage. The native-position 576 × 690 charts combine locally damp rock, rare warm iron and discontinuous pale inclusions; they use the archived native colour underneath with exact neutral limits, a roughness response, and shallow optical normal relief only.

**Do not treat an empty prop list as empty ground.** The actual current `copse-floor.glb` Git blob `7dc9cf345efbec44ec5cd1f0c24dadb7ed32a706` already places **9,456** low forest-floor triangles in this window. Three existing native tiles contain them; 359 original vertices lie within eight metres of the registered eye. The nearest was measured at 4.805 m in plan. `underlay.py` freezes their conservative projected footprint and a 20 cm safety margin instead of proposing duplicate roots, moss or ferns. The actual central meadow-sward region is elsewhere. Future woodland source must respect this held footprint.

The prepared full-stage Blender recipe is `atelier.py -- NATIVE STAGED_TRUE_TERRAIN HELD_COPSE_FILE WATER_INPUTS SITE_OUTPUT PHOTOGRAPHS`; `layout.py` selects L0 native terrain through both the true forest shelf and its 240 m peak. Both photographed states, when executed, retain the same stock marine sky, original y=0 sea, actual surrounding source props, and original copse foliage. Do not call these future pictures finished: so far this is an independently editable source and a frozen map of the actual cameras.

**Bough-derived floor proposal.** Sampling the actual alpha-tested green needles on 22 mapped mature spruce around the full study produced 10,202 accepted original bough samples. Gravity plus the native slope puts warm needlefall on just 4.73% of the window. The opt-in layer respects the existing copse; 88 small spent-cone seats fall outside the conservative old-floor keep-out. The portable cone forms are author source only until `cones.py SITE_OUTPUT` runs and the result is inspected. `canopyfall.py NATIVE SITE_OUTPUT` generates the measured transfer and register. The optional `with-registered-floor` argument for the full Blender stage adds this work to the rock contacts, requests the separate body-eye geometry, and draws zero cone meshes for the guardian. It never makes gameplay objects.

## The photographed desktop study: three true slope shoulders

The initial full desktop composition exposed a different defect from the broad, bland rock: three dark picket-like forms seemed to float. They are **actual installed Cliff props #1335, #1695, and #1847**, not the terrain skyline. At the summit-view pixel (343,84) of the 672 × 400 source, the original visible surface is specifically #1695 at (57.830,124.532,−352.160); the peak above it is original habitat tile 5/3, some 100 m higher. The largest height excess on #1695's actual zero-height base vertices is **7.67 m**, versus 9.8 m among all its lower two local metres. The base gaps on #1335 and #1847 reach 6.22 m and 4.07 m.

The proposed stone keeps each record's real pivot, yaw, scale, oriented 8 × 4 local metre footprint and original obstacle top (103.976, 135.033, 81.726 m respectively). Instead of a level plinth it runs into the actual ground: every perimeter sample, 84 near or 44 far per outcrop, sits 12 cm below its native V1 triangle. The broad shoulder and two lower broken beds use their own vertical mapped quartz, gneiss and iron pigment. Warm broken granite now crosses the intervening true slopes, while steeper local fall lines tie each foot into the surrounding mantle. **Nothing was registered into the game; the terrain, baked obstacles, thirteen-metre constraint on the tallest visual prop, and physical behaviour remain original.** The seated result is a narrow standing face, not a claim that the old proxy is naturally broad or that close creature collision has been tried.

**Geometry and files.** Keep the matching external `jointed-rock-face.png` beside either `slope-shoulders-L0.glb` (three placements, 832 triangles each) or `slope-shoulders-L1.glb` (224 each). They are alternatives, not two simultaneous sets or replacement global Cliff assets. The original local Cliff mesh had 728 triangles at L0. The creature window instantiates just 11 spent cones (2,454 triangles); the whole editable kit holds 88, and the guardian draws zero. Existing 9,456 native copse triangles and all the original trees stay. The 171 × 183 metre, 650 × 695 broken-granite chart is optically exposed on 35.29% of this mountain region by its coverage criterion, not a change to its heights. The two GLB LOD files are directly editable in a normal GLTF-aware modelling tool. No workflow execution is required.

**How to read the gallery.** `register/summit-window-matched.jpg` is a creature's 1.04 m eye facing the true mountain. `register/shelf-section-matched.jpg` is the matched, 173 m-wide guardian lens. Left is original, right is the art proposal. `register/sheltered-boles-matched.jpg` is the separate, downward-looking needlefall lens from the same original eye. These are **bounded software drawings of the actual archived scene meshes, not game screenshots or Blender renders**. They hold the original marine sky mesh, actual conifers, rocks, alpha-cutout native forest floor, directional sun, two ground-shadow maps and all camera transforms. The renderer uses source diffuse texels, vertex pigment and simple diffuse illumination with air falloff; it does not claim native BRDF, full normal-map lighting, wind, moisture or FPS. The held y=0 ocean uses the original V1 optical water chart so a gap in the low tree line is not filled by an invented blue backdrop. The optional full Blender stage has not been run for this shelf.

A native adopter must replace only the three specified graphic instances and choose the proper LOD without duplicating props, mounts or ground. Native contact, visible transition and frame-cost review belong to the later gameplay window.
