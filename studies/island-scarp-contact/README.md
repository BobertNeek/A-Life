# Sixteen planted V1 escarpments: a separate editable art proposal

[Creature eye at actual landmark 1549](scarp-eye.jpg) · [guardian at actual foothill 1731](scarp-spur.jpg) · [high notch 1395](rift-root.jpg) · [original geometric diagnosis](original-and-joined-cast.jpg). All are Blender photographs, not a running game.

The new shoulder and joined stone replace pencil points at the two normal landmarks. At 1549 the dark air slit below the unseated block is gone; it still reads as an isolated four-metre-wide outcrop on a plain slope. The severe 1395 notch remains visibly cantilevered, not a solved ground contact. Its original terrain supports only a small uphill portion; the gallery deliberately shows that failure. Keep this entire pack as a proposal rather than substituting it silently on the production branch.

[Open the compact editable Blender library](planted-fault-scarps.blend) to choose any of the sixteen original placement collections. The original source is retained in its own collection. The packaged [multi-scene glTF](seated-cliffs.glb) needs the three files in the adjacent [minerals/](minerals/) directory; the Blender file packs them. The original fallbacks remain the three named Cliff_L* meshes.

The original cliff was a stockade of sharpened posts. A generic solid replacement cleaned the crown, but exposed a ruler-straight, hanging sole where the frozen terrain fell below it. Affected by everything from 0.69 m to 40.48 m at the downhill edge; it is not a texture defect. **No false pillars are extended below the native obstacle.** The upper footprint and summit stay put; lower surfaces retreat toward real uphill contacts. The sixteen original world positions, collision fixtures, terrain and saves are untouched.

The receipt contains actual original anchors, placement transforms and per-level counts. Aggregate GLB: 5977732 bytes, including the original fallback; the sixteen proposed models share native mineral images. At the three photographed sites, near meshes have 2485, 2480, 2406 triangles. That is an asset tally, not a frame-cost result.
