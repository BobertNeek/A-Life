# Sunspindle Hollow

[Matched folio](gallery/Sunspindle-Hollow.jpg) · [Packed Blender project](gallery/Sunspindle-Hollow.blend) · [Four original lossless photographs](gallery/locked-photographs.zip)

At 1.04 m a previously bare trough now has pale split seed cups within reach, overlapping silver mats, and taller copper stands receding up the floor. Fine soil shows through the gaps. From above, the valley reads as several interlocking copper and sage patches rather than a uniform pale oval; the guardian change is quieter than the eye-level one and the exposed hillside above it is intentionally unaltered. These are matched offline Blender photographs, not gameplay.

This is a new eastern island site, not an extension of the two Weatherlip stones. The basin already exists behind the old foredune. Its profile bottoms near +1.75 m above the original water plane; no water was painted into the dry pocket. At each half-metre of north/south travel the authoring pass samples the original V1 cross-slope for its lowest point; the smoothly interpolated *habitat response* is not a replacement mesh or a route. [Every second metre of the original section](gallery/sections.csv) is retained. Original scree #1311 and boulders #1648, #1454 and #1400 remain as placed.

Cream sunspindle bracts protect ochre seeds beside low silver-leaf mats. Copper rush concentrates on the deposited fans; a few woody windwillows hold the drier shoulders. The bare intervals are part of the meadow, not a drawn gameplay path. The new plant roots are keyed to actual V1 triangles. Original saved obstacles were excluded using the native Xmin/Zmin/Xmax/Zmax column order and expanded crown clearance margins. No new rigid obstacles, rocks, water, dirt skirts, wildlife or crops. Wind, movement, collision and organism interaction are not represented by this offline scene.

The ground wash is one world-space RGBA chart: peat, leaf sheath, pale silt fan and dry grass. Actual plant density and fallen sheaths influence the pigment within a stand; the older mineral surface deliberately survives in its gaps. A separate 1.78 m seamless plate supplies split awns, grit and shallow relief at creature scale. Both layers bind to the original material and normal chain on the actual island in Blender. No second ground sheet or displacement. The existing coastal sunlight, tree positions, V1 geometry and old material outside the painted reach stay as before. All photographs use the same 1000×600 frame and 48 samples before/after. Tree/ground LOD differs only between the two distances, not the sides of a pair.

## Reopen, not install

The .blend contains the identical V1 topology of the site excerpt, a packed colour chart and its two micro-surface plates, the individually editable registered clumps with linked forms and both cameras. The remote hills, ordinary habitat props and sky are only in the full photographic shoot, not painted into this standalone workbench. The ordinary native source, former cove and earlier studies remain intact.

The two GLBs are source candidates with explicitly registered glTF instancing. They share exactly the same planting register. A 23 m near band around the creature camera uses anatomically cut bracts, branched awns and folded leaves. Outside it, and across the guardian version, each plant uses an opaque silhouette. Those are **station-specific art exports**, not an installed or moving LOD system. The [root ledger](gallery/root-register.csv) and [site manifest](gallery/site.json) include counts, true camera/landmark coordinates, and triangle totals. Small leaves, pigment and relief are not collision geometry. No engine build, gameplay run, validation CI, performance test, user PC or main changed.
