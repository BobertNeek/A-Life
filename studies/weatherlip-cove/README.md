# Weatherlip Cove

[Three matched photographs](gallery/Weatherlip-Cove.jpg) · [Editable Blender source](gallery/Weatherlip-Cove.blend) · [Exact native cross-sections](gallery/native-sections.csv)

The rocky northern head, the twin-stone bench and the lower southern beach belong to the same **337.43 m open bight** on the original western coast. This is not a made-up inlet or a flattened gallery stage. The native sea-level intercept curves from about X −399 m to −346 m and back toward −351 m. From Z −103 m to Z 225 m, the V1 triangle bed transitions from more than 19 m of ascent within 15 horizontal metres to about 3 m within the same distance. A long, uninterrupted beige-green coastal strip used to conceal that change in material and exposure.

At guardian height the previously continuous beige-green shore now separates into an exposed northern face, breaks of warm sand and lower grey-green tongues down-coast. At the unchanged 1.04 m eye the previously smooth rock toe shows genuine mineral seams, scattered settled chips and small rooted plants against open water; sheltered ground remains distinct and the two original erratics remain recognisable. The brush is spare and the old angular boulders still cast a pronounced dark gap; this coastal pass neither reshapes those bodies nor claims to cure their contact. The crown of the headland stays as smooth as its source V1, rather than receiving a false taller silhouette.

## How it is built

The grey-green, occasionally iron-warmed bedrock is a **pigment and shallow normal change on original ground**. The shapes respond to actual height, slope and the exact dry/wet edge. Long contacts break where native sand intervenes. The larger relief and headland silhouette are the island itself; we do not paint black lines and pretend they are a new cliff.

The closest thin edges are actual cosmetic 3D forms: 580 fitted pieces share 116 irregular rock rafts, with narrow broken joints between neighbours; lower scattered chips are mostly smaller than a foot. A 512² pigment/relief pair supplies physical quartz flecks, hairline weathering, moisture and a raised grain at 1.38 m and 28.1 cm scales. A separate, earth-addressed rock/sand/turf mask keeps that fabric out of bare sand and grass. Bare sand instead receives low shell grit and short washed ripples at its own scales. The materials fade out entirely outside the cove. Flatter ground caps authored stone lift at 3.8 cm at its vertices; on steeper non-walkable slopes the bound is 7.5 cm. The original shell and dune bodies, old alders and rock transforms are not moved. Three different plant habits share the actual sheltered places: glaucous fescue, low fleshy coastal rosettes and shell-pink thrift. Wind-shorn saltbrush sits farther inland. Low broken wrack ribbons occur in native swash pockets, not along an invented border. This is visual evidence only, not a collision, walkability or performant-loading claim.

## Files and scope

- `gallery/Weatherlip-Cove.blend` is a portable artist workbench: exact cut of the original V1 triangle pairs, packed editable world-space chart, 3D brush, wrack and stone, real landmark reference empties and the three frozen cameras. Its neutral workbench is **not** a gameplay scene or the source of the photographs; the pictures separately use the full island, actual stock landmarks, selected original foliage/water and the same daylight in each pair.
- `gallery/weatherlip-ground.png` is the 787 × 2,266 RGBA chart at approximately 15.6 cm per texel. `site.json` gives native X/Z bounds, shelf parameters, exact camera poses, stock landmark transforms and exported artist counts. `native-sections.csv` stores a real sea-level intersection every 2 m along Z plus unchanged slopes sampled at four cross-shore offsets.
- `gallery/broken-beds.glb` contains the piecework and strand ribbons; `gallery/living-margins.glb` contains the rooted colonies. Both carry original native Y-up metre coordinates. The exported stone GLB carries its vertex pigments; the full world-metre rind shader is in the Blender file and material source, not embedded as portable glTF terrain UVs. The grouped source model can be edited in Blender without the full game checkout.
- `shore.py`, `forms.py` and `atelier.py` regenerate the optional art from the pinned original surface and saved props; `stations.py` is the sole three-camera definition. `material.py` layers the chart onto existing material normals, not an invented mesh landscape. The existing offshore art studio produced the original controls, final artwork and editable cut. `gallery/capture.json` records exact source, photo hashes and native tree provenance. Neither the base terrain, its physical footprint, the gameplay branch nor any renderer binding changes with this study.
